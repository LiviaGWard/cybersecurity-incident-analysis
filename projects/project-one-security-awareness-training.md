# Project One: Security Awareness Training Case Study
**CYB-200 | Cybersecurity Foundations | Grade: A**

## Scenario Summary
Analyzed a healthcare organization (Stellar Technologies) facing multiple security vulnerabilities: an unpatched third-party software product (MyPatientRecords via TakeMyData), a conflict-of-interest vendor relationship involving the CISO's son, and a culture of suppressing incident reporting.

---

## A. Security Gaps — Human Factors
- **Third-party risk awareness:** Employees lacked training to assess and escalate vendor security risks. The unpatched MyPatientRecords software represented an overlooked attack vector that could expose patient data.
- **Conflict of interest recognition:** No training existed to help employees identify or report decisions influenced by personal relationships rather than security needs.

## B. Security Gaps — Legal Factors
- **HIPAA compliance:** The organization lacked adequate training on data protection obligations, creating exposure to regulatory penalties and legal liability.
- **Incident reporting:** Employees were directed *not* to inform stakeholders of the Stellar Technologies vulnerability — directly contradicting legal disclosure requirements and allowing the vulnerability to persist.

## C. Proactive Security Mindset Recommendations
A proactive security posture means security is embedded into operations — not reactive. Recommendations included:
- Regular third-party vendor audits with documented security assessments
- Anonymous reporting channels to surface conflicts of interest without fear of retaliation
- Continuous security awareness training covering legal obligations, not just technical threats
- A culture where employees feel empowered to escalate concerns
